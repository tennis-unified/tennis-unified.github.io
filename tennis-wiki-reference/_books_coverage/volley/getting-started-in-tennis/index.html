RQ The volley is a short stroke with lit- tle or no backswing. The first moves (Figs. 1 and 2) should be toward the oncoming shot. The sooner you meet the ball, the more weight you'll put in the shot and the sooner it will get back over the net. The finish (Fig. 3) shows that the stroke has been made with the racket head sliding under the ball. This puts underspin on the shot for greater control. Notice where the player's left hand is in Fig. 2. He’s really using that free hand to keep the head of the racket from going back too far and getting behind his body.

A balanced ready position is the starting point for getting to each ball quickly and ef- ficiently in tennis. At the baseline (LEFT), awaiting serve or taking up your position between shots, your knees should be bent, weight centered on the balls of the feet, and racket held comfortably in front of the body, pointing at the net. The ready position is the same at net (BE- LOW) except that the racket head is held much higher. That makes it easier to hit down on the ball with your volley stroke and also speeds up your reactions because the racket is at eye level.
