ART-555: Returning the Kick Serve: Positioning and Timing Adjustments

Executive Summary

The kick serve bounces high and can push the returner behind the baseline, making it challenging to return effectively. This article covers the positioning, timing, and technique adjustments needed to return kick serves at all levels.

The Challenge of Returning Kick Serves

High Bounce

  • Kick serves bounce 4-8 feet at the service line (shoulder height or above)
  • The high bounce forces the returner to hit the ball above the strike zone
  • Hitting above waist height reduces power and control

Heavy Topspin

  • Kick serves generate 2500-4500 RPM of topspin
  • The topspin causes the ball to kick up sharply after the bounce
  • The spin makes the ball dive in flight, requiring the returner to judge the trajectory carefully

Backing Up

  • The high bounce often pushes the returner behind the baseline
  • Being deep behind the baseline reduces the ability to attack
  • Returners must decide whether to stay back or step in

Positioning for Kick Serves

Starting Position

  • Start slightly further back than for flat or slice serves
  • This allows you to handle the high bounce without being pushed too deep
  • Be ready to adjust forward or backward based on the serve's trajectory

On Clay

  • Start even further back (6-10 feet behind baseline) because kick serves bounce highest on clay
  • Be prepared to hit the ball at shoulder height or above
  • Use topspin returns to handle the high bounce

On Hard Courts

  • Start 3-6 feet behind the baseline
  • The bounce is high but not as extreme as on clay
  • You can step in more aggressively to take the ball earlier

On Grass

  • Start closer to the baseline (1-3 feet behind)
  • Kick serves bounce lower on grass due to the slippery surface
  • Be ready to stay low if the bounce is lower than expected

Timing Adjustments

Wait Longer

  • Kick serves take longer to bounce and reach their peak
  • Do not rush the return — wait for the ball to come down to a comfortable height
  • Patience is key to effective kick serve returns

Step In or Stay Back?

  • Step in: If you can take the ball before it reaches its peak, you can attack
  • Stay back: If the ball bounces too high, stay back and hit it on the way down

The Two-Step Adjustment

  1. First step: Move backward to create space for the high bounce
  2. Second step: Step forward into the return as the ball descends

Technique for Returning Kick Serves

High Forehand Return

When the kick serve bounces above shoulder height: 1. Use a semi-western or western grip for more topspin 2. Swing from low to high to lift the ball over the net 3. Aim deep crosscourt for safety 4. Do not try to hit through the ball — use topspin to control the trajectory

High Backhand Return

When the kick serve bounces above shoulder height: 1. For one-handed backhands: Use a continental grip; swing from low to high 2. For two-handed backhands: The topspin grip helps control the high bounce 3. Aim deep crosscourt 4. Accept that you cannot hit with full power at this height

The Shoulder-High Block

When the kick serve bounces at shoulder height: 1. Use a short, compact swing 2. Block the ball back with a firm wrist 3. Aim crosscourt to neutralize the serve 4. Use the server's pace to redirect the ball

Returning Kick Serves to Different Targets

Wide Kick Serve

  • The high bounce makes it difficult to hit a sharp angle
  • Block the ball back crosscourt
  • Recover to the center after the return

Body Kick Serve

  • The high bounce jams you, making it hard to swing
  • Use a compact block
  • Aim crosscourt or at the server

T Kick Serve

  • You have more time because the serve is down the middle
  • Use a full swing to drive the return
  • The high bounce is easier to handle when you are not pulled wide

Tactical Considerations

Neutralizing the Kick Serve

  • Block the ball back crosscourt
  • Focus on getting the ball deep
  • Do not try to hit winners off kick serves

Attacking the Kick Serve

  • Step in to take the ball before it reaches its peak
  • Drive the ball aggressively when you catch it at the right height
  • Target the server's weaker side

The Lob Return

  • A lob can neutralize a high-bouncing kick serve
  • The lob buys time to recover your position
  • Effective against servers who rush the net

Common Faults

Hitting Into the Net

  • The high bounce causes the ball to drop rapidly
  • If you swing too early, you hit into the net
  • Fix: Wait longer; let the ball come down to a comfortable height

Hitting Long

  • Trying to hit through a high-bouncing ball causes the ball to sail long
  • Fix: Use topspin to control the trajectory; aim deeper in the court

No Power

  • Hitting above waist height reduces power generation
  • Fix: Step in to take the ball earlier; use a more compact swing for better leverage

Practical Application: "The High-Bounce Drill"

Have a partner stand at the service line and toss balls high in the air, simulating the bounce of a kick serve. Practice returning these balls at shoulder height, focusing on: 1. Waiting for the ball to come down to a comfortable height 2. Using topspin to control the trajectory 3. Aiming deep crosscourt

This drill develops the patience and technique needed for returning high-bouncing kick serves.
