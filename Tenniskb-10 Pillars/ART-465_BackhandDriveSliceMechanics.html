ART-465: High Backhand Volley

Executive Summary

The high backhand volley is an offensive shot that allows the player to hit down on the ball and finish the point. It requires a firm wrist, a slightly closed racket face, and a compact swing. Mastering this shot allows the player to handle high balls effectively and finish points at the net.

When to Use the High Backhand Volley

The high backhand volley is used when the ball is above net height and the player is at the net. This situation commonly arises when the opponent hits a high, floating return or when the player is approaching the net behind a high shot.

The high volley is an offensive shot, as the player can hit down on the ball and finish the point. The goal is to hit the ball with pace and precision, directing it to a difficult position for the opponent.

Technique for the High Backhand Volley

The high backhand volley requires the player to hold the racket high, with the racket head above the wrist. The racket face should be slightly closed, allowing the player to hit down on the ball. The swing is short and punchy, with the racket meeting the ball in front of the body.

The player should use a firm wrist, preventing the racket from collapsing under the force of the ball. The follow-through should be short, with the racket finishing around shoulder height.

Body Position for the High Volley

The body position for the high volley requires holding the racket high, with the racket head above the wrist. The player should be balanced and ready to move in any direction. The racket should be held out in front of the body, with the racket face slightly closed.

Players who fail to hold the racket high often struggle to hit down on the ball, resulting in a weak, floating shot. Developing the habit of holding the racket high is essential for an effective high volley.

Common High Volley Errors

One common error is using an open racket face, resulting in a weak, floating shot. Another is using a loose wrist, resulting in a loss of control. Some players also use a long swing, resulting in a loss of control and an increased risk of error.

Players who struggle with the high volley often benefit from drills that emphasize a firm wrist and a compact swing. Practicing the high volley with a focus on these elements helps develop the technique needed for an effective shot.

Drills for the High Volley

Several drills help develop the high volley. The "high volley drill" involves having a partner feed high balls and practicing the volley, focusing on a firm wrist and a compact swing. The "wall drill" involves hitting high volleys against a wall, focusing on the racket face and the firm wrist. The "transition drill" involves hitting an approach shot and then moving forward to hit a high volley, developing the full transition game.

Players should incorporate these drills into their regular practice routine, focusing on developing the technique needed for an effective high volley.

Practical Application: "The High Volley Practice"

Stand at the net and have a partner feed high balls to your backhand side. Practice hitting ten high backhand volleys, focusing on a firm wrist, a slightly closed racket face, and a compact swing. Count how many volleys land in the court and how many penetrate the court. Track progress over sessions to ensure the high volley is developing correctly.
