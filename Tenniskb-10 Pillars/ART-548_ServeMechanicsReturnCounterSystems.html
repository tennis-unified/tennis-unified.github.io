ART-548: Serve Match Preparation: Scouting Opponent Return Patterns

Executive Summary

The best servers do not just serve — they serve with a plan based on the returner's tendencies. This article provides a framework for scouting opponent return patterns and using that information to optimize serve strategy.

Pre-Match Scouting

Watching Previous Matches

If you can watch video of your opponent's previous matches, note: - Where do they stand to return on the deuce court? - Where do they stand to return on the ad court? - Do they favor crosscourt or down-the-line returns? - How do they handle different serve types (flat, slice, kick)? - What happens when you serve to their backhand vs. forehand?

Talking to Other Players

If other players have faced your opponent: - Ask about their return strengths and weaknesses - Ask where they struggled most - Ask what serve types were most effective

Warm-Up Observations

During the warm-up before the match: - Watch how the returner positions themselves - Note their grip (forehand or backhand grip for returns) - Observe their footwork (do they step in or stay back?) - Pay attention to their body language (confident or tentative?)

In-Match Scouting

The First Service Game

Your first service game is a scouting opportunity: - Hit one serve to each target (wide, body, T) - Note where the returner positions themselves - Observe which returns are strongest and weakest - Do not worry about winning this game — gather information

Tracking Return Patterns

Mentally track: - Return position: Does the returner stand in the same place or shift? - Return direction: Does the returner always hit crosscourt? Down the line? - Return quality: Which serve types produce the weakest returns? - Return errors: Which serve types force the returner into errors?

The Scoring System

Mentally rate each return: - +1: Weak return that you can attack - 0: Neutral return that leads to a rally - -1: Strong return that puts you on the defensive

After 3-4 service games, you should have a clear pattern of which serves produce the best results.

Exploiting Return Weaknesses

Weak Backhand Return

  • Serve to the backhand 70-80% of the time
  • Use kick serves that bounce high to the backhand
  • Occasionally serve to the forehand to prevent cheating

Weak Forehand Return

  • Serve to the forehand 60-70% of the time
  • Use slice serves that pull them wide to the forehand
  • Occasionally serve to the backhand to prevent cheating

Poor Handling of High Bounces

  • Use kick serves to both sides
  • Target the body with kick serves
  • Use heavy topspin on second serves

Poor Handling of Low Balls

  • Use slice serves that stay low
  • Use flat serves that skid through
  • Serve with less spin to keep the bounce low

Slow Footwork

  • Serve wide to force lateral movement
  • Vary placement to keep them moving
  • Use fast serves to take time away

Adjusting Strategy Mid-Match

If Your Primary Pattern Is Working

Continue using it but add occasional variation to prevent the returner from adjusting.

If the Returner Adjusts

If the returner starts covering your primary target: - Switch to the opposite side - Change serve types (if you were using slice, switch to kick) - Add more body serves to jam them

If You Are Being Broken Frequently

  • Slow down and serve more conservatively
  • Target the returner's weaker side more aggressively
  • Use more spin to reduce double faults
  • Take extra time between points to reset mentally

Diagnostic & Training Matrix

Observation Failure Result Corrective Strategy
No pre-match scouting No opponent awareness Serving without a plan Pre-match observation; video review
Same serving pattern regardless of opponent No tactical adjustment Predictable serving Opponent-specific planning
Returner consistently attacks second serves No second serve adjustment Broken games Second serve development; placement adjustment
Cannot identify returner weaknesses Poor observation Missed opportunities Scoring system; pattern tracking
Strategy not working but no adjustment Inflexible approach Continued poor results Mid-match adjustment; pattern breaking

Practical Application: "The Returner Report Card"

After the first set, mentally grade the returner's returns: - Forehand return: Strong / Average / Weak - Backhand return: Strong / Average / Weak - High bounce handling: Good / Average / Poor - Low ball handling: Good / Average / Poor - Return position: Aggressive / Neutral / Passive

Based on these grades, develop your serving strategy for the second set: - Target the weak side more - Use the serve type they handle worst - Adjust placement based on their return position

This simple mental exercise ensures you are serving with a plan, not just hitting balls.
