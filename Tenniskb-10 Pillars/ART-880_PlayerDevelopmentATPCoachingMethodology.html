ART-880: Compression Garments and Recovery Wear in Tennis

Executive Summary

Compression garments—including sleeves, shorts, tights, and socks—are widely used by tennis players for both performance enhancement and recovery. These garments apply graduated pressure to the limbs, theoretically improving blood flow, reducing muscle oscillation, and accelerating recovery. While research evidence is mixed, many players report reduced muscle soreness, improved proprioception, and enhanced recovery when wearing compression garments during and after competition. When used as part of a comprehensive recovery program, compression garments can complement other strategies including hydration, nutrition, and active recovery.

Types of Compression Garments

Compression Sleeves

  • Applied to the forearm or upper arm
  • Target: Elbow and forearm support
  • Use: During play (tennis elbow prevention) and post-match recovery
  • Pressure: 15-30 mmHg

Compression Shorts/Tights

  • Applied to the thigh and hip area
  • Target: Quadriceps, hamstrings, glutes
  • Use: Post-match recovery
  • Pressure: 15-30 mmHg

Compression Socks

  • Applied to the calf and lower leg
  • Target: Calves, shins, ankles
  • Use: During travel (DVT prevention) and post-match recovery
  • Pressure: 15-30 mmHg

Full-Body Compression

  • Top and bottom combination
  • Target: Full-body recovery
  • Use: Post-match recovery, travel
  • Pressure: 15-30 mmHg

Proposed Benefits of Compression Garments

Performance Benefits

Proposed Benefit Evidence Level Description
Reduced Muscle Oscillation Moderate Less muscle vibration during movement
Improved Proprioception Moderate Better joint position sense
Reduced Muscle Fatigue Weak to Moderate Delayed onset of fatigue
Improved Blood Flow Mixed Increased venous return
Reduced Soreness Moderate 20-30% reduction in DOMS

Recovery Benefits

Proposed Benefit Evidence Level Description
Reduced Muscle Soreness Moderate Decreased perceived soreness
Faster Waste Removal Weak Enhanced lactate clearance
Reduced Swelling Moderate Decreased edema
Improved Recovery Perception Moderate Subjective improvement in recovery
Reduced Injury Risk Weak Limited evidence for prevention

Evidence Summary

What the Research Shows

Moderate Evidence Supports: - Reduced muscle soreness after exercise - Improved proprioception - Reduced swelling

Mixed or Limited Evidence For: - Improved performance during play - Faster lactate clearance - Reduced injury risk - Improved strength recovery

Why Players Use Them

Despite mixed evidence, many players report: - Feeling more supported during play - Less muscle soreness after matches - Improved recovery perception - Psychological comfort and confidence - Placebo effect that enhances recovery

Compression Garment Protocols

During Play

Forearm Sleeve: - Wear during play for elbow support - Choose 15-20 mmHg pressure - Ensure it does not restrict wrist movement - Useful for players with tennis elbow history

Compression Socks: - Wear during play for calf support - Choose 15-25 mmHg pressure - Help reduce calf fatigue in long matches - Popular during hot conditions

Post-Match Recovery

Full-Body Compression: - Wear for 2-4 hours post-match - Choose 15-30 mmHg pressure - Combine with hydration and nutrition - Most effective when worn immediately post-match

Compression Tights: - Wear for 2-4 hours post-match - Target quadriceps, hamstrings, glutes - Reduce muscle soreness in primary tennis muscles

Travel

Compression Socks: - Wear during long flights (4+ hours) - Reduce risk of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) - Minimize ankle swelling - Promote circulation during sedentary travel

Practical Application: "The Compression Garment Schedule"

During Match: - Forearm sleeve (if elbow issues) - Compression socks (for calf support)

Post-Match (immediately): - Full-body compression garment - Wear for 2-4 hours - Combine with hydration, nutrition, cool-down

Travel: - Compression socks during flights - Change every 4-8 hours - Move regularly during travel

Sleep: - Compression tights (optional) - May improve overnight recovery - Comfort is the priority

Diagnostic & Training Matrix

Observation Failure Result Corrective Strategy
Persistent muscle soreness Insufficient recovery, no compression Reduced next-day performance Post-match compression; 2-4 hours wear
Elbow pain during play Insufficient support, poor technique Tennis elbow symptoms Forearm sleeve; technique review; equipment adjustment
Calf fatigue in long matches Insufficient support, poor conditioning Reduced movement quality Compression socks; calf conditioning; hydration
Leg swelling after travel Insufficient movement, no compression Discomfort, reduced recovery Compression socks during travel; movement during flights
Slow recovery between matches Insufficient recovery protocols Cumulative fatigue Full-body compression post-match; comprehensive recovery
