ART-354: Elbow Position Through the Hitting Zone

Executive Summary

The elbow's position through the hitting zone—its height, distance from the body, and angle of flexion—is a critical variable that affects both power generation and directional control in the modern forehand. Elite players demonstrate a characteristic elbow trajectory: the elbow starts low and close to the body at the set position, rises and moves forward through the hitting zone, and finishes high and extended after contact. This trajectory optimizes the kinetic chain's energy transfer while maintaining the arm's mechanical advantage throughout the swing.

Elbow Position at the Set Position

At the set position, the elbow is typically:

  1. Low: The elbow is at or below shoulder level, with the forearm angled downward toward the court.

  2. Close to the body: The elbow is within 0.2 to 0.4 meters of the torso, maintaining the arm's connection to the trunk.

  3. Flexed: The elbow is bent to 70 to 110 degrees (double-bend forehand) or nearly extended (straight-arm forehand).

This position stores elastic energy in the shoulder and elbow muscles, preparing them for the explosive contraction that drives the forward swing.

Elbow Trajectory Through the Hitting Zone

As the forward swing progresses through the hitting zone, the elbow follows a characteristic trajectory:

  1. Rising: The elbow rises from its low set position to approximately shoulder height at contact, driven by the shoulder's abduction and the trunk's rotation.

  2. Moving forward: The elbow moves from its close-to-body position to 0.3 to 0.5 meters in front of the torso, extending the arm toward the contact point.

  3. Flexing or extending: In the double-bend forehand, the elbow maintains its flexion (70 to 110 degrees) through contact. In the straight-arm forehand, the elbow extends to 150 to 170 degrees at contact.

Elbow Height and Shot Type

The height of the elbow at contact affects the shot type:

  1. Low elbow (below waist level): Produces a steep low-to-high swing path, generating heavy topspin but less forward drive. Used for defensive shots and low-bouncing balls.

  2. Mid elbow (waist to shoulder level): Produces a moderate swing path, balancing topspin and forward drive. Used for standard rally balls.

  3. High elbow (above shoulder level): Produces a level or downward swing path, generating less topspin but more forward drive. Used for attacking high balls.

Elbow Distance and Control

The distance of the elbow from the body at contact affects control:

  1. Close to body (less than 0.3m): Reduces the lever length, increasing control but decreasing power. Used for precise shots.

  2. Moderate distance (0.3 to 0.5m): Balances power and control. The standard position for most forehands.

  3. Far from body (more than 0.5m): Increases the lever length, increasing power but reducing control. Used for aggressive shots where power is prioritized.

Diagnostic & Training Matrix

Observation Failure Result Corrective Strategy
Elbow too low at contact Steep swing path; excessive topspin Balls landing short Raise elbow; adjust swing path angle
Elbow too high at contact Level or downward swing path Low topspin; flat shots Lower elbow; steepen swing path
Elbow too close to body Restricted swing; reduced power Weak shots Extend arm more; increase elbow distance
Elbow too far from body Overextended; reduced control Directional errors Reduce extension; bring elbow closer
Elbow drops during forward swing Loss of power transmission Weak, pushed shots Maintain elbow height through hitting zone

Practical Application: "Elbow Awareness"

During practice, consciously monitor your elbow position at three key moments: (1) at the set position, (2) at contact, and (3) at the finish. Note the height, distance from body, and angle of flexion at each point. This awareness helps you develop a consistent elbow trajectory that optimizes power and control for your individual mechanics.
