ART-816: Cardiovascular Conditioning for Tennis

Executive Summary

Tennis cardiovascular conditioning must address the sport's unique energy demands: intermittent high-intensity efforts interspersed with brief recovery periods. Matches require a strong aerobic base for recovery between points, anaerobic capacity for sustained high-intensity rallies, and repeated sprint ability for the explosive movements that determine point outcomes. Effective conditioning programs periodize aerobic development during the off-season and shift to high-intensity interval training during the pre-season and in-season phases.

Energy System Demands in Tennis

Aerobic System

  • Contribution: Provides the majority of energy (60-70%) during a match
  • Role: Recovery between points, sustained performance over 2-5 hour matches, multi-day tournament play without performance decay
  • Key Metric: VO2max (aerobic capacity)
  • Elite Range: 50-65 mL/kg/min for male ATP players

Anaerobic System

  • Contribution: Provides energy for high-intensity efforts (30-40% of match)
  • Role: Explosive first steps, serves, overheads, and high-intensity rallies
  • Key Metrics: Peak power output, anaerobic capacity, lactate tolerance
  • Elite Range: Blood lactate levels of 6-12 mmol during intense rallies

ATP-PC System

  • Contribution: Provides immediate energy for 5-10 second explosive efforts
  • Role: Serves, overheads, first-step quickness
  • Key Metric: Peak power output
  • Recovery: 3-5 minutes for full replenishment

Aerobic Base Building

Off-Season Aerobic Development

Frequency: 3-4 sessions per week Duration: 30-60 minutes per session Intensity: 60-75% of maximum heart rate (conversational pace)

Aerobic Training Methods

  1. Steady-State Running: 30-60 minutes at moderate intensity
  2. Cycling: Low-impact alternative for aerobic development
  3. Swimming: Non-impact aerobic training with upper body involvement
  4. Rowing: Full-body aerobic training with low impact
  5. Cross-Training: Elliptical, stair climber, or similar modalities

Aerobic Benchmarks

Level VO2max (mL/kg/min) 3 km Run Time 1.5 Mile Run Time
Recreational 35-45 14-18 min 12-15 min
Competitive Junior 45-55 11-14 min 9-12 min
Professional 50-65 9-12 min 7-10 min

Anaerobic Capacity Development

Pre-Season Anaerobic Training

Frequency: 2-3 sessions per week Intensity: 85-95% of maximum heart rate

Anaerobic Training Methods

  1. Interval Running: 400-800 meter repeats at 5K pace with equal rest
  2. Hill Sprints: 10-15 second uphill sprints with walking recovery
  3. Tempo Runs: 20-30 minutes at lactate threshold pace (comfortably hard)
  4. Circuit Training: High-intensity circuits with minimal rest

Anaerobic Benchmarks

Level 400m Time Blood Lactate (post-match)
Recreational 80-100 sec 3-5 mmol
Competitive Junior 65-80 sec 5-8 mmol
Professional 55-70 sec 6-12 mmol

Repeated Sprint Ability (RSA)

RSA is the ability to perform repeated sprints with minimal performance decrement—critical for tennis where 3-5 explosive movements may occur per point with only 20-30 seconds of rest.

RSA Training Protocols

Protocol 1: Short Sprints - 10 x 20-meter sprints - 20-second rest between sprints - Focus: First-step quickness and acceleration

Protocol 2: Tennis-Specific Sprints - 10 x baseline-to-net sprints (10-15 meters) - 30-second rest between sprints - Serve shadow after each sprint - Focus: Serve-and-volley transition

Protocol 3: Court Coverage - 6 x court-width lateral sprints - 30-second rest between sprints - Focus: Lateral movement speed

Protocol 4: Rally Simulation - 4 x 45-second high-intensity on-court drills - 90-second rest between drills - Focus: Sustained high-intensity output

On-Court Conditioning Drills

The Suicide Drill

  • Sprint from baseline to service line and back
  • Sprint to net and back
  • Sprint to opposite service line and back
  • Repeat 3-5 times with 60-second rest between sets
  • Focus: Court coverage speed and repeated sprint ability

The 10-Ball Drill

  • Coach feeds 10 balls rapidly to alternating sides
  • Player must return each ball to a target
  • 30-second rest, repeat 4-6 sets
  • Focus: High-intensity rallying with incomplete recovery

The Shadow Rally

  • Player executes full movement patterns (split-step, move, stroke, recovery)
  • Continuous for 45-60 seconds
  • 60-second rest, repeat 4-6 sets
  • Focus: Match-like movement intensity

The Serve-and-Recover Drill

  • Player serves and transitions to the net
  • Coach feeds a passing shot
  • Player volleys, recovers to baseline, serves again
  • Continuous for 10 serves
  • Focus: Serve-and-volley conditioning

In-Season Maintenance

Goal: Maintain cardiovascular fitness without adding fatigue.

Frequency: 2-3 sessions per week (lower volume than off-season) Duration: 20-40 minutes per session Intensity: Mix of moderate and high-intensity work

Sample In-Season Week

Day Session Type Duration Intensity

Tuesday RSA training 20 min High (85-95% HRmax)
Wednesday Active recovery 20 min Low (60% HRmax)
Thursday Anaerobic intervals 25 min High (85-95% HRmax)

Monitoring Cardiovascular Fitness

Resting Heart Rate

  • Measure daily upon waking
  • Elevated RHR (5+ bpm above baseline) suggests incomplete recovery or overtraining
  • Decreasing trend over weeks suggests improving aerobic fitness

Heart Rate Recovery

  • Measure heart rate immediately after exercise and 1 minute post-exercise
  • Faster recovery (30+ bpm decrease in 1 minute) indicates better aerobic fitness
  • Slower recovery suggests fatigue or declining fitness

VO2max Estimation

  • Cooper Test: 12-minute run distance estimates VO2max
  • Beep Test: Multi-stage fitness test estimates VO2max
  • Regular testing (every 8-12 weeks) tracks fitness progression

Practical Application: "The Tennis Fitness Test Battery"

Establish a standardized fitness test battery to monitor progress:

  1. Aerobic Fitness: 1.5-mile run or 12-minute Cooper run
  2. Speed: 20-meter sprint (flying start)
  3. Agility: Illinois Agility Test
  4. Repeated Sprint Ability: 10 x 20-meter sprints with 20-second rest
  5. Upper Body Strength: Maximum push-ups in 60 seconds
  6. Core Strength: Maximum plank time

Test at the start of off-season, mid off-season, end of pre-season, and mid-season. Use results to adjust training focus.

Diagnostic & Training Matrix

Observation Failure Result Corrective Strategy
Performance decline in third sets Insufficient aerobic base, poor lactate clearance Fatigue, errors, reduced court coverage Aerobic base building; lactate tolerance training
Slow recovery between points Poor aerobic fitness, inadequate cardiovascular conditioning Cumulative fatigue, reduced performance Aerobic training; HRV monitoring; recovery protocols
Inability to maintain intensity Poor repeated sprint ability, insufficient anaerobic conditioning Performance drop in long matches RSA training; interval training; on-court conditioning drills
Heavy legs in matches Insufficient lower body conditioning, poor movement efficiency Reduced court coverage, slower first step Lower body conditioning; movement efficiency training
