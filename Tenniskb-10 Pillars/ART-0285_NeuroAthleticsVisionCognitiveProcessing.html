ART-0285: Pre-Performance Routines in Tennis

Executive Summary

Pre-performance routines are consistent sequences of physical and mental actions performed before executing a skill. In tennis, routines before serving, returning, and between points enhance focus, confidence, and consistency by creating a familiar, controllable process. Developing and practicing pre-performance routines is one of the most effective mental skills training techniques.

Components of a Pre-Performance Routine

Physical Actions

  • Bouncing the ball before serving
  • Adjusting strings or clothing
  • Specific foot positioning
  • Taking a deep breath

Mental Actions

  • Visualizing the shot
  • Using a cue word or phrase
  • Focusing on the tactical goal
  • Controlling arousal through breathing

Temporal Structure

  • The routine has a consistent duration
  • Each action follows a predictable sequence
  • The routine ends with a clear "go" signal

Benefits of Pre-Performance Routines

Enhanced Focus

Routines direct attention to task-relevant cues, preventing distraction and maintaining focus.

Increased Confidence

The familiarity of the routine creates a sense of control and confidence.

Consistent Arousal

Routines help maintain optimal arousal by providing a predictable, calming structure.

Automatic Execution

With practice, the routine becomes automatic, reducing the cognitive load of preparation.

Pressure Resistance

Routines are particularly effective under pressure because they provide structure when anxiety threatens to disrupt performance.

Developing a Pre-Performance Routine

Identify Your Current Patterns

Observe what you naturally do before serving or returning. Build on these natural patterns.

Keep It Simple

The routine should be simple enough to execute consistently. A complex routine is difficult to maintain under pressure.

Practice Under Pressure

Practice the routine in pressure situations to ensure it becomes automatic and resilient.

Personalize

The routine should feel natural and comfortable. What works for one player may not work for another.

Diagnostic & Training Matrix

Observation Failure Result Corrective Strategy
Inconsistent preparation No routine Variable performance Develop consistent routine
Rushed preparation Anxiety-driven speed Poor execution Include calming elements
Cannot maintain routine under pressure Routine breakdown Choking Practice routine under pressure
Routine is too long Consumes time Disruption Simplify and shorten

Practical Application: "The Serve Routine"

Develop a consistent serve routine: (1) stand behind the baseline, (2) take one deep breath, (3) visualize the serve landing in the target, (4) bounce the ball three times, (5) use your cue word ("smooth"), (6) execute the serve. Practice this routine in every practice session until it becomes automatic. During matches, use this routine to create a familiar, controllable process that enhances focus and confidence. The serve routine is the most important pre-performance routine in tennis because every point begins with your serve.
