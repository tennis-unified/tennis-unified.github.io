ART-535: Serve and Court Positioning: Post-Serve Placement

Executive Summary

The serve does not end at contact — what happens in the 1-2 seconds after the serve determines whether you control the point or are caught out of position. This article covers post-serve court positioning based on serve type, placement, and the returner's tendencies.

The Recovery Process

Step 1: Land and Stabilize

After contact, land on the front foot and stabilize your balance. Do not rush the landing — a stable landing sets up better recovery movement.

Step 2: Push Off

Push off the landing foot to begin moving toward the ready position. The direction of the push depends on where you served.

Step 3: Move to the Ready Position

Move to the optimal court position based on the serve placement. Get there as quickly as possible while staying balanced.

Step 4: Prepare for the Return

Assume a ready position (knees bent, racket in front, weight on balls of feet) and prepare for the return.

Post-Serve Positioning by Serve Placement

After a Wide Serve

  • Direction of recovery: Toward the center of the court
  • Why: The returner will likely hit crosscourt to the open court; you need to cover that angle
  • Position: 1-2 feet inside the baseline, toward the center from the middle of the court
  • Preparation: Expect a crosscourt return; position to cover that angle

After a Body Serve

  • Direction of recovery: Toward the center of the court
  • Why: The return is likely to be short or down the middle
  • Position: Center of the court, slightly inside the baseline
  • Preparation: Expect a variety of returns; stay balanced and ready

After a T Serve

  • Direction of recovery: Toward the center of the court
  • Why: The T serve splits the court, so the return could go either way
  • Position: Center of the court, at the baseline or slightly inside
  • Preparation: Expect a return to either side; stay balanced and react

Post-Serve Positioning by Serve Type

After a Flat Serve

  • The return will likely be faster (flat serves give the returner less time but also less spin to handle)
  • Position slightly deeper to handle the pace
  • Expect a more aggressive return

After a Slice Serve

  • The return will likely be lower (slice serves stay low)
  • Position slightly closer to the baseline to handle the low bounce
  • Expect a crosscourt return to the open court

After a Kick Serve

  • The return will likely be higher (kick serves bounce high)
  • Position slightly deeper to handle the high bounce
  • The returner may have difficulty attacking, so expect a more defensive return

Anticipation Based on Returner Tendencies

Returner Who Always Hits Crosscourt

  • Position slightly toward the crosscourt angle
  • This "cheating" gives you a head start on the most likely return

Returner Who Mixes Returns

  • Stay in the center and react
  • Do not commit early to either side

Returner Who Attacks Second Serves

  • Position slightly closer to the baseline on second serves
  • Be ready for an aggressive return

Returner Who Stays Deep

  • Position slightly deeper as well
  • The return will have more pace but less angle

The Ready Position After Serving

Body Position

  • Knees slightly bent
  • Weight on the balls of the feet
  • Racket held in front of the body, at chest height
  • Eyes on the returner's racket

Mental State

  • Anticipate the return
  • Be ready to move in any direction
  • Focus on the returner's racket face (it tells you where the ball is going)

Common Post-Serve Positioning Faults

Standing Still

Failing to move after serving leaves you in the serving position, which is not the optimal position for the next shot.

Committing Too Early

Moving to one side before the return is struck allows the returner to hit to the open court.

Wrong Distance from Baseline

Standing too close to the baseline makes you vulnerable to deep returns. Standing too far back makes you vulnerable to short returns.

Poor Balance

Landing off-balance delays recovery movement and leaves you vulnerable to any return.

Diagnostic & Training Matrix

Observation Failure Result Corrective Strategy
Standing still after serving No recovery movement Caught out of position Recovery movement drills; serve-and-move practice
Committing to one side early Poor anticipation Returner hits open court Anticipation drills; stay-central practice
Wrong distance from baseline No adjustment for serve type Vulnerable to returns Distance adjustment drills; serve-type positioning practice
Off-balance after serving Poor landing mechanics Slow recovery Landing mechanics drills; balance training
Always positioned in same spot No tactical adjustment Predictable positioning Tactical positioning drills; returner analysis

Practical Application: "The Cone Recovery"

Place a cone in the optimal recovery position for each serve type. After each serve, sprint to the cone and assume the ready position. Time yourself — aim for under 2 seconds from landing to ready position. This drill develops the habit of immediate recovery movement and helps you find the optimal position for each serve type.
