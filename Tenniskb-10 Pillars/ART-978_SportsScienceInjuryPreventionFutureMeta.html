ART-978: Tennis Analytics — The Rise of Data-Driven Tennis

Executive Summary

Tennis has entered the analytics revolution, with advanced statistics moving beyond simple winners and errors to sophisticated metrics like shot quality scores, expected point value, and win probability added. These data-driven insights are transforming how players prepare for opponents, make in-match tactical decisions, and evaluate their own performance.

The Evolution of Tennis Statistics

Traditional Statistics

  • First serve percentage
  • Aces and double faults
  • Winners and unforced errors
  • Break point conversion rate
  • Net approaches won/lost

Limitations of Traditional Stats

  • Context-blind (a winner at 30-0 counts the same as at break point)
  • No quality measure (a forced error vs. a gift error counted the same)
  • No spatial component (where on court was the shot hit?)
  • No tactical insight (what patterns led to the point outcome?)

Advanced Analytics

  • Shot quality: Composite measure of speed, spin, and placement
  • Expected point value: Probability of winning the point based on shot quality and positioning
  • Win probability added: How each shot changes the probability of winning the match
  • Pattern analysis: What shot sequences lead to success
  • Pressure performance: Performance in high-leverage situations

Shot Quality Metrics

What is Shot Quality?

Shot quality is a composite measure that combines: 1. Speed: Faster shots reduce opponent's reaction time 2. Spin: More spin creates more difficult returns 3. Placement: Shots closer to lines and corners are harder to return 4. Depth: Deeper shots push opponent back, reducing their options 5. Context: Court position and situation (offensive vs. defensive)

Expected Value (EV)

Every shot in tennis has an expected point value: - EV = Probability of winning the point given the current shot quality and positioning - A perfect serve to the corner: EV = 0.85 (85% chance of winning the point) - A weak mid-court ball: EV = 0.30 (30% chance of winning the point)

Win Probability Added (WPA)

  • Measures how a shot changes the probability of winning the match
  • Example: A winner on break point might add 15% to win probability
  • Example: An unforced error on set point might subtract 20% from win probability

Match Charting

What is Match Charting?

Manual or automated recording of every shot in a match: - Shot type (serve, forehand, backhand, volley, overhead) - Direction (crosscourt, down-the-line, middle) - Outcome (winner, forced error, unforced error, in play) - Location (where on court was the shot hit from and to?) - Speed and spin (if technology available)

Tennis Abstract

  • Crowdsourced match charting project
  • Database of thousands of matches with detailed shot-level data
  • Public access to advanced statistics
  • Foundation for much of tennis analytics research

Tennis Data

  • Professional match charting service
  • Provides data to teams, broadcasters, and researchers
  • Detailed shot-level analysis
  • Real-time and historical data

Key Analytical Insights

Serve Patterns

  • Serve +1: The shot immediately after the serve is critical
  • Serve placement: Correlation between serve placement and point outcome
  • Second serve patterns: What do players do on second serves?
  • Return positioning: How does returner positioning affect serve effectiveness?

Rally Patterns

  • Crosscourt vs. down-the-line: Which is more effective from different positions?
  • Rally length: What is the optimal rally length for different player types?
  • Pattern recognition: What shot sequences lead to winners vs. errors?
  • Defensive transitions: What shots help transition from defense to offense?

Tactical Patterns

  • Attack the backhand: Does it work? Under what circumstances?
  • Net approaches: When is approaching net most effective?
  • Lob usage: When is the lob most effective?
  • Drop shot: When is the drop shot worth attempting?

Pressure Performance

  • Break point conversion: Who performs best under pressure?
  • Tiebreak performance: Who wins the big points?
  • Comeback ability: Who performs best when behind?
  • Closing ability: Who closes out matches most effectively?

Data-Driven Tactical Planning

Pre-Match Preparation

  • Review opponent's analytical profile:
  • Preferred serve placements
  • Most common rally patterns
  • Weaknesses under pressure
  • Performance by surface and conditions
  • Develop tactical plan based on data

In-Match Adjustments

  • Track patterns during the match:
  • Is the opponent targeting your backhand?
  • Are you winning enough second-serve points?
  • Are your net approaches successful?
  • Adjust tactics based on what the data shows

Post-Match Analysis

  • Review match data:
  • What patterns were successful?
  • Where were points lost?
  • What adjustments would improve outcomes?
  • Use data to guide training priorities

Analytical Tools and Platforms

Professional Tools

  • Tennis Data: Professional match charting and analytics
  • SAP Tennis Analytics: WTA official analytics platform
  • Hawk-Eye: Ball tracking and player positioning
  • SwingVision: AI-powered match analysis for amateurs

Amateur Tools

  • Tennis Keeper: App-based match statistics
  • Tennis Stats: Manual stat tracking
  • YouTube video analysis: Self-review with video
  • SwingVision: Automated video analysis

Limitations of Tennis Analytics

Data Quality

  • Manual charting is time-consuming and prone to error
  • Automated systems may not capture context (wind, score, fatigue)
  • Sample sizes may be small for meaningful conclusions

Context

  • Numbers do not tell the full story
  • Psychological factors are difficult to quantify
  • Match situations vary (playing with lead vs. behind)
  • Opponent quality varies

Implementation

  • Players may not know how to use analytics
  • Data overload can be paralyzing
  • Analysis takes time and expertise
  • Not all insights are actionable

Diagnostic & Training Matrix

High unforced error rate on backhand Technical or tactical weakness Targeted by opponent Technical review; tactical adjustment to protect backhand
Declining first serve percentage in later sets Fatigue or loss of confidence Reduced serve effectiveness Serve technique review; conditioning; tactical adjustment

Short rally length (most points <4 shots) Overly aggressive or passive play Inconsistent results Analyze rally length by outcome; optimize aggression level

Practical Application: "Start With One Number"

You do not need a team of analysts to benefit from tennis analytics. Start tracking one number in your matches: first serve percentage. After five matches, you will likely notice patterns: your percentage drops in the third set, or it is lower against certain opponents. This single insight can lead to actionable changes: better toss consistency, more spin on second serves, or more aggressive first-serve placement. Once you are comfortable with one number, add winners-to-unforced-error ratio, then break point conversion. Small, consistent analytical habits compound into significant performance improvements over time.
