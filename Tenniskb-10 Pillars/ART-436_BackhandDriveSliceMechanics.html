ART-436: Two-Handed Backhand Slice

Executive Summary

The two-handed backhand slice is a valuable defensive and transitional shot, though less common than the one-handed slice. It uses a Continental grip in the bottom hand and a neutral grip in the top hand, producing a stable, low slice. Mastering this shot adds versatility to the two-handed backhand game.

Grip for the Two-Handed Slice

The two-handed backhand slice typically uses a Continental grip in the bottom hand (non-dominant for a right-handed player) and a neutral or slightly closed grip in the top hand. The bottom hand's Continental grip provides the open racket face needed for slice, while the top hand stabilizes and guides.

Some players shift the top hand toward Continental as well, producing a more open face and higher slice. Others keep the top hand in an Eastern forehand grip, producing a flatter, more driving slice. Players should experiment to find the combination that produces the best slice.

The Slice Swing Path

The two-handed backhand slice uses a high-to-low swing path, similar to the one-handed slice. The racket starts above the contact point and descends through the ball, brushing the back and underneath. This descending path is what creates backspin.

The two-handed slice may have a slightly shallower descent than the one-handed slice, as the extra hand provides more control. This can produce a flatter, more penetrating slice, which is useful for offensive situations.

Contact Point for the Two-Handed Slice

Contact for the two-handed slice occurs in front of the body, typically at chest to shoulder height. The racket face is tilted open, and the ball contacts the strings on the upper portion of the string bed because the racket is descending through contact. The follow-through continues downward and slightly forward, finishing around waist height.

The two-handed slice contact point is typically slightly further forward than the one-handed slice, as the extra hand provides additional reach. Players should adjust the contact point based on the incoming ball height and the desired slice trajectory.

Generating Backspin with Two Hands

Backspin is generated by the racket face brushing down and under the ball. The combination of the open racket face and the descending swing path creates the spin. The bottom hand's Continental grip is the primary driver of the open face, while the top hand helps guide the racket.

The amount of backspin depends on racket head speed through contact and the angle of the swing path. Players who want to increase backspin should focus on accelerating the racket through the contact zone and steepening the swing path.

Defensive Two-Handed Slice

The defensive two-handed slice is used when the player is out of position or facing aggressive pace. It uses a high, floating trajectory with heavy backspin to slow down the rally and buy time for recovery. The swing path is steep, the racket face is wide open, and the follow-through is long and upward.

The defensive slice is particularly effective for players who prefer the two-handed backhand for drives, as it allows them to stay in their preferred style without switching to a one-handed slice.

Offensive Two-Handed Slice

The offensive two-handed slice uses a shallower path and less open face. The ball stays low, skids through the court, and takes time away from the opponent. This shot is effective on low balls and as a change of pace during rallies.

The offensive slice is less common than the defensive slice, as many players prefer to hit a drive or a one-handed slice in offensive situations. However, it can be a useful weapon for players who are comfortable with it.

Common Two-Handed Slice Errors

One common error is failing to maintain the open racket face, resulting in a flat shot with little backspin. Another is swinging too slowly, resulting in a floaty ball that does not skid. Some players also struggle with the top hand, allowing it to dominate and close the racket face.

Players who struggle with the two-handed slice often benefit from drills that emphasize the open racket face and the descending path. Practicing the swing with a focus on these elements helps develop the technique needed for an effective slice.

Practical Application: "The Two-Handed Slice Practice"

Stand on the baseline and hit ten two-handed slice backhands, focusing on producing a low, skidding bounce. After each shot, note whether the ball stays below net height after the bounce. If it bounces high, the racket face is too open or the path is too steep. Adjust until at least seven of ten shots skid low, building the feel for an offensive two-handed slice.
