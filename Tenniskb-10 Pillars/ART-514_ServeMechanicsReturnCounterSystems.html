ART-514: Second Serve Reliability: The 70 Percent Threshold and Beyond

Executive Summary

The second serve is often the most undertrained yet most critical serve in tennis. A reliable second serve — one that goes in at least 70% of the time while still preventing the returner from attacking — is the foundation of holding serve consistently. This article covers the biomechanics of the second serve, the 70% threshold principle, and strategies for developing a second serve that holds up under pressure.

The 70% Threshold Principle

Statistical analysis of professional tennis has established that a first-serve percentage of 65-70% is associated with winning the majority of service games. Below 55%, the server becomes increasingly vulnerable to being broken. The threshold exists because:

  • Each 1% improvement in first-serve percentage corresponds to approximately 0.5-0.8% improvement in service games won
  • Second serves are won at a significantly lower rate than first serves (typically 45-55% vs. 65-75%)
  • Double faults — which occur only on second serves — give free points to the returner

Second Serve Biomechanics

The second serve differs from the first serve in three key biomechanical ways:

1. More Spin, Less Pace

Second serves typically generate 30-50% more topspin than first serves while traveling 20-30% slower. The increased spin creates: - Greater net clearance (reducing double faults) - Higher bounce (making the serve harder to attack) - More control over direction

2. Higher Toss

The toss for a second serve is typically placed higher and more over the head than for a first serve, facilitating the upward brushing motion needed for topspin.

3. Longer Loading Phase

Second serves often have a slightly longer loading phase at the backscratch, allowing more time to generate spin through the brushing motion.

Types of Second Serves

Kick Serve (Most Common)

The heavy topspin kick serve is the standard second serve at all levels. It provides: - High net clearance (3-6 feet above the net) - High bounce (shoulder height or above at the service line) - Moderate pace (70-90 mph at the club level, 90-110 mph at the professional level)

Slice-Spin Hybrid

Combines sidespin with topspin, creating a serve that both kicks up and curves laterally. This adds an extra dimension of difficulty for the returner but requires more precise mechanics.

Underhand Second Serve (Rare)

In specific situations (deep returner, windy conditions), an underhand serve can be a reliable second serve option. However, this is uncommon at higher levels and may be perceived as unsportsmanlike.

Developing a Reliable Second Serve

The "Safety Zone" Approach

Develop a second serve that you can make 90% of the time at 70% of your maximum effort. This serve should: - Clear the net by at least 2-3 feet - Land in the middle third of the service box - Generate enough topspin to bounce above waist height at the service line

Once this "safety zone" serve is reliable, gradually increase pace while maintaining the same spin rate.

The "No Double Fault" Rule

During practice, establish a personal rule of never double-faulting. If you miss your first second serve attempt, take something off the second attempt to ensure it goes in. This builds the habit of reliability over aggression.

Pressure Simulation

Practice second serves under pressure conditions: - Simulate serving at deuce with the score at 30-40 - Play "second serve only" practice games where every serve must be a second serve - Practice serving after intense physical exercise to simulate match fatigue

The Psychology of the Second Serve

Many players develop a fear of double-faulting that causes them to hit even slower, weaker second serves. This creates a vicious cycle: - Fear of double fault → weaker second serve → returner attacks → pressure increases → fear increases

The solution is to: 1. Develop confidence through repetition (hit 100+ second serves daily) 2. Focus on spin and placement rather than speed 3. Trust your mechanics and commit fully to the serve

Practical Application: "The 100-Ball Challenge"

Every practice session, hit 100 second serves with the goal of making at least 85 of them. Track your success rate over time. If you consistently achieve 85%+, begin increasing the pace of your second serves by 5% each week until you find the speed threshold where your percentage drops below 80%. Your optimal second serve speed is just below that threshold — fast enough to challenge the returner but reliable enough to avoid double faults.
