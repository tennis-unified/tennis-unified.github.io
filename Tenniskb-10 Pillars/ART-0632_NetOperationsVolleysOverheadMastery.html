ART-0632: The Volley Toss — Creating Your Own Attack Opportunities

Executive Summary

The volley toss is a soft, arcing volley hit upward to buy time when you are caught in a defensive position at the net. It is a neutral-to-offensive shot that turns a defensive situation into a potential attacking opportunity by giving you time to recover position and forcing the opponent to generate their own pace. While less well-known than other volley variations, the volley toss is a valuable tool for advanced net players.

When to Use the Volley Toss

The volley toss is appropriate when: (1) you are at the net but off-balance or out of position, (2) the incoming ball is too hot to redirect effectively, (3) you need time to recover to the center of the court, (4) the opponent is in a strong attacking position and you want to neutralize their advantage. It is not a primary shot — it is a recovery tool that buys time and resets the point.

Technical Execution

The volley toss uses the continental grip with an open racket face (approximately 30-45 degrees). The swing path is upward with minimal forward drive — you are lifting the ball rather than driving it. Contact occurs at approximately waist height, 12-18 inches in front of the front foot. The follow-through is upward and slightly toward the target. The key is to hit the ball high enough (15-25 feet in the air) that you have time to recover position.

Height and Depth Considerations

The volley toss should reach a height of 15-25 feet, giving you approximately 2-3 seconds to recover. The depth should be sufficient to prevent the opponent from hitting an easy smash — aim for the service line or deeper. If the toss is too short, the opponent can smash it. If it is too low, you do not gain enough recovery time. The optimal toss is high and deep, landing near the baseline.

The Volley Toss as a Transition Shot

After hitting a volley toss, use the recovery time to get back to the center of the court and into a ready position. The opponent must now generate their own pace to hit an overhead or groundstroke from the baseline. This shifts the momentum — you are no longer defensive, and the opponent must create their own attack. Follow the toss by closing to the net if the opponent hits a weak reply.

Common Volley Toss Errors

Hitting the toss too low — insufficient recovery time. Hitting the toss too short — the opponent can smash it easily. Telegraphing the toss with a different preparation — the opponent anticipates and closes the net. Hitting the toss with too much forward drive — it becomes an easy smash. Not recovering after the toss — wasting the time the toss was supposed to buy.

Tactical Application

Use the volley toss to: (1) recover from a defensive position at the net, (2) neutralize hard passing shots that you cannot redirect, (3) buy time to get back to the center of the court, (4) force the opponent to generate their own pace, and (5) reset the point when you are off-balance. In doubles, the volley toss is useful when the net player is pulled wide and needs time for their partner to cover the open court.

The Volley Toss vs. the Defensive Lob

The volley toss and the defensive lob serve similar purposes (buying time), but the volley toss is hit before the ball bounces (from the net) while the defensive lob is hit after the bounce (from the baseline). The volley toss is faster to execute because you are already at the net, but it requires more touch because you are hitting from closer to the net. Choose the volley toss when you are already at the net; choose the defensive lob when you are at the baseline.

Volley Toss Drills

The "defensive volley toss" drill practices the toss from off-balance positions at the net. The "recovery volley toss" drill practices the toss followed by recovery to the center of the court. The "volley toss height" drill rewards tosses that reach a target height. The "volley toss competition" game incorporates the toss into competitive net play.

Summary

The volley toss is a recovery tool for net players who are caught in a defensive position. By hitting a high, soft volley that lands deep, you buy time to recover and force the opponent to generate their own pace. While not a primary shot, the volley toss is a valuable addition to the advanced net player's toolkit.

Practical Application: "Buy Time, Recover Position"

The volley toss is about buying time. When you are caught defensive at the net, hit a high, deep toss that gives you 2-3 seconds to recover. Then close to the net and attack the opponent's reply. The toss is not the point — the recovery is.
