Singles Tactical Patterns & Shot Selection

The doubles side of this site has patterns and formations. Singles deserves the same treatment.

Why This Matters

You can own great technique and still lose to a player with worse strokes but a clearer game plan. Singles tennis rewards players who know which of four "phases of play" they're in on any given ball, and who have 2-3 go-to patterns for each phase, rather than reinventing a plan point by point.

Core Concepts

The four phases of play.

  • Attack — you have a short, high, or weak ball; the point is yours to finish.
  • Build — a neutral rally ball; your job is to construct advantage, not force a winner.
  • Neutral — both players are in position; patience and consistency dictate.
  • Defense — you're out of position or under pressure; the only goal is to survive the point and reset to neutral.

Most rec-level errors come from playing an "attack" shot selection while actually still in "build" or "neutral" — going for a winner too early because the ball felt hittable, not because the phase justified it.

Repeatable shot patterns, not just isolated great shots. A pattern is a 2-3 shot sequence you can execute on command: for example, deep crosscourt forehand > short ball response > inside-out forehand winner. Recreational players who improve fastest tend to drill 3-4 patterns until they're automatic, rather than trying to have unlimited shot options.

Scouting-informed game plans. Before or early in a match, identify one clear weakness (weak second serve, poor movement to the ad-side backhand corner, discomfort at net) and build your shot selection around exploiting it consistently, rather than playing "your game" regardless of opponent.

Action Steps

  1. Before your next match, write down (mentally or literally) 3 patterns you'll try to execute — not just "hit deep" but the full 2-3 shot sequence.
  2. During warm-up, identify your opponent's weaker wing and commit to directing 60%+ of rally balls there in neutral/build phases.
  3. Practice phase recognition in drilling: have a partner or coach call out "attack / build / neutral / defense" mid-rally and match your shot selection to the call, not to how the ball feels.

Common Mistakes

  • Going for winners from neutral or defensive positions because the shot "felt good," not because the phase called for it.
  • Playing the same pattern regardless of what the scouting/warm-up revealed about the opponent.
  • Treating singles tactics as "just hit it where they're not," with no repeatable sequence behind it.

Quick Reference

Phase Ball You Got Goal
Attack Short/high/weak Finish the point
Build Neutral, slight advantage possible Construct, don't force
Neutral Even rally ball Consistency, patience
Defense Under pressure/out of position Survive, reset
