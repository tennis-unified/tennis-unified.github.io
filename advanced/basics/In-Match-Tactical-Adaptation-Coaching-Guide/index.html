In-Match Tactical Adaptation Coaching Guide¶

The follow-up skill to Scouting: what to do when your pre-match plan stops working.

Why This Matters¶

Scouting builds your plan before and early in a match. This guide covers the separate skill of noticing, mid-match, that the plan needs to change — the most common reason a player who "had a game plan" still loses is failing to adapt when the opponent adjusts.

Core Concepts¶

Signals that a plan has stopped working. Losing the same point pattern three times in a row, an opponent visibly changing their positioning or shot selection after you've exploited a weakness once, or your own execution breaking down under a style you can't sustain physically — all are cues to adjust, not push through unchanged.

Adjust one variable at a time. Effective in-match adaptation changes one clear thing — target a different wing, change the shot's height/spin, change your court position — rather than scrapping the entire game plan out of frustration. Wholesale changes under pressure tend to produce worse tennis, not better.

The changeover is your adaptation window. Use the 90-second changeover deliberately: identify what changed, name one adjustment, and commit to testing it for the next game rather than re-deciding point by point.

Action Steps¶

  1. Track patterns in threes. If you lose the same type of point three times in a row, that's your signal to change something specific on the fourth attempt, not to keep repeating it hoping for a different result.
  2. Use changeovers as checkpoints, not just rest. Ask: what's working, what stopped working, what's one adjustment to test next game.
  3. Adjust the least costly variable first — court position or shot placement before a wholesale style change, since smaller adjustments are easier to execute reliably under match fatigue.
  4. Distinguish "my execution failed" from "my plan failed." If you're missing an open-court shot you normally make, the issue may be fatigue or nerves, not tactics — don't abandon a good plan because of a bad execution stretch.

Common Mistakes¶

  • Continuing to run a plan for a full set after it has clearly stopped working, out of stubbornness or lack of a backup plan.
  • Overreacting to one lost point by abandoning an otherwise-sound game plan.
  • Making several changes simultaneously under pressure, making it impossible to tell which adjustment actually helped.

Quick Reference¶

Signal Likely Cause Adjustment
Same pattern lost 3x Opponent has solved your plan Change one variable, test next game
Opponent repositions after a weakness is exploited They've adjusted Find the next-best target
Execution breaking down, not tactics Fatigue/nerves Simplify, don't overhaul the plan
