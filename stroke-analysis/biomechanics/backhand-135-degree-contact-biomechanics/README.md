# One-Handed Backhand Biomechanics: The 135° Contact Angle, Full Arm Extension & Rotational Kinetic Drive

This directory contains the comprehensive biomechanical analysis of the modern one-handed backhand (1HBH) contact point geometry and kinetic chain, based on high-speed cinematography of Roger Federer and Stan Wawrinka.

## Core Principles
1. **$135^\circ$ Forearm-to-Racket Handle Angle ($90^\circ$ vertical + $45^\circ$ drop):** Keeps the wrist in anatomical neutral, locking the joint against collision shock and maximizing topspin brushing.
2. **Full Arm Extension at Contact ($180^\circ$ straight elbow):** The arm transitions from a flexed preparation ($90^\circ-110^\circ$) to a straight arm at impact, maximizing effective lever radius ($r$) and tangential head speed ($v = \omega r$).
3. **Body Rotation Powers Arm Extension ("Not Arming the Ball"):** Forward swing is driven by pelvic-lumbar uncoiling and ground reaction forces, centrifugally flinging the arm into extension.
4. **Follow-Through: Non-Rotational Forearm with Elbow Tucking Inside Chest:** The forearm does not pronate. The elbow tucks in front of the sternum to guide the strings vertically for pure low-to-high WSWM topspin brushing.
