Ground Reaction Forces (GRF): Vertical vs. Horizontal Vectors

> PRO CUE: Power is not generated by the arm; it is harvested from the ground through a perfectly timed leg drive, transferred across a stiffened core, and released through long-axis rotation of the shoulder.

Mechanical Foundations

The kinetic chain in high-performance tennis represents a continuous mechanical conduit. Peak velocity at racket impact is achieved through the sequential summation of speed from proximal (heavy, slow body segments) to distal (light, fast limb segments). Energy leaks occur whenever a segment fails to stiffen at the exact millisecond of kinetic transfer, forcing smaller distal joints (wrist, elbow) to compensate.

Step-by-Step Kinematic Sequence

1. Ground Loading: Flex ankles, knees, and hips to convert downward body mass into Ground Reaction Force ($GRF_{vertical} \ge 2.0 \times \text{Body Weight}$).

2. Pelvic Snap: Uncoil the pelvis toward the ball while the upper torso remains coiled, expanding the pelvic separation angle.

3. Torso Stiffness Pulse: Contract the abdominal obliques dynamically to lock the core, transforming the spine into a rigid torsional rod.

4. Scapular & Shoulder Release: Allow the upper arm to lag behind torso rotation before triggering humerus long-axis rotation.

5. Impact Point Snap: Decelerate proximal rotation rapidly to whip the racket head forward into the impact window.

Segmental Velocity Contribution Matrix

| Kinetic Segment | Biomechanical Action | Velocity Contribution (%) | Peak Phase Window |

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| Legs / Hips | Quad Drive & Pelvic Rotation | 20% - 25% | $t_0 \to t_1$ (Loading to Uncoiling) |

| Trunk / Core | Oblique Sling Torsion & Stiffness Pulse | 30% - 35% | $t_1 \to t_2$ (Max Separation Angle) |

| Shoulder / Arm | Humerus Long-Axis Internal Rotation | 35% - 40% | $t_2 \to t_3$ (Acceleration Phase) |

| Wrist / Hand | Radiocarpal Stabilization & Impact Release | 5% - 10% | $t_3 \to \text{Impact}$ (Terminal Snap) |

> MECHANICAL DEFECT WARNING: Initiating racket swing with the arm before pelvic drive causes severe kinetic leakage, reducing ball velocity by up to 35% and drastically increasing risk of elbow tendinopathy.
