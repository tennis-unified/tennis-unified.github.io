> PRO CUE: Turn the shoulders and hips as a single unit using the non-dominant hand on the racket throat; strike the ball well in front of the forward hip.

Mechanical Foundations

The modern forehand relies on an early, synchronized shoulder-pelvis rotation (Unit Turn) to store torsional potential energy across the core. By establishing impact geometry in front of the body, the player maximizes the lever arm while protecting the shoulder joint from posterior impingement forces.

Step-by-Step Kinematic Execution

1. Unit Turn Preparation: Initiate turn via non-dominant hand pushing racket throat back while rotating hips 45° and shoulders 90°.

2. Lag Phase: Drop racket head into slot position below ball level as forward leg drive commences.

3. Forward Contact Point: Drive racket face into ball contact $12 - 18\text{ inches}$ in front of the lead hip at waist height.

Forehand Contact Geometry Matrix

| Contact Zone | Leverage & Force Transfer | Joint Load Safety | Racket Face Control |

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| Behind Body Axis | Severe Kinetic Leakage | High Elbow / Shoulder Stress | Open / Unstable Face |

| In Front of Body (Optimal) | Max Kinetic Transmission | Protected Joint Alignment | Locked Square Face |

> MECHANICAL DEFECT WARNING: Pulling the racket back with the arm alone isolates arm muscles, destroys kinetic chain sequence, and leads to late impact behind the hip plane.
