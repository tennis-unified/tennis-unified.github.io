Returning to Starting Positions (Recovery)¶

Recovery is the most neglected but most critical footwork skill. You can't hit the next ball if you're not in position.

Why Recovery Matters¶

  • Prevents being caught out of position
  • Enables consistent shot quality
  • Forces opponent to hit better shots
  • Controls court geometry
  • Saves energy - efficient movement

Recovery Principles¶

Principle Action
Immediate Start recovery AS contact is made
Efficient Shortest path to tactical center
Balanced Arrive in ready position
Anticipatory Adjust for opponent's tendencies

Recovery Patterns by Shot¶

Baseline Rally Recovery¶

  1. Finish stroke - follow through complete
  2. Cross-over step - first step with outside foot
  3. Side-shuffle - quick lateral steps to center
  4. Split step - as opponent contacts ball
  5. Ready position - balanced, racket up

After Approach Shot¶

  1. Follow through toward net
  2. Continue forward - don't stop
  3. Split step at service line area
  4. Volley position - racket up, knees bent

After Volley¶

  1. Hold position or move forward
  2. Racket stays up - ready for next
  3. Small adjustments - shuffle steps
  4. Cover most likely return

After Serve¶

  1. Land on front foot
  2. Immediate split step (if staying back)
  3. Move to baseline center
  4. Or move in (serve-and-volley)

After Return¶

  1. Follow through toward target
  2. Recover to baseline center
  3. Or move in on weak second serve
  4. Split step as server hits

The Cross-Over Step¶

The single most important recovery move:

Wrong:  Side-shuffle all the way back (slow, hips turn)
Right:  Cross-over step FIRST, then side-shuffle
  • First step: Outside foot crosses in front
  • Covers 6-8 feet in one step
  • Hips stay square to net
  • Then side-shuffle for fine positioning

Tactical Center vs Geometric Center¶

Center Type Position When to Use
Geometric Exact middle of baseline Never
Tactical Center of opponent's likely returns Always

Tactical center shifts based on: - Your shot placement (crosscourt → shade crosscourt) - Opponent's strengths (shade to weak side) - Score/pressure (more conservative)

Recovery Drills¶

1. Hit and Recover¶

  • Coach feeds, you hit, immediate recover to center
  • Coach feeds to different spots
  • Focus on first move = cross-over step

2. Two-Ball Recovery¶

  • Hit first ball, recover, hit second ball
  • Second ball fed to opposite side
  • Builds recovery speed

3. Pattern Recovery¶

  • Specific pattern: FH crosscourt → recover → BH down line → recover
  • Grooves tactical recovery

4. Live Ball Recovery¶

  • Rally with partner
  • Rule: Must split step every ball
  • Rule: Must cross-over step first recovery move

5. Video Check¶

  • Record match play
  • Watch: Do you recover immediately?
  • Check: First move = cross-over?

Common Recovery Errors¶

Error Consequence Fix
Admire shot Late start Eyes on opponent, not ball
Side-shuffle only Slow, hips turn Cross-over step FIRST
Wrong center Open court Tactical, not geometric
No split step Late reaction Split step EVERY opponent contact
Stop short Caught in no-man's land All the way to tactical center

Recovery Fitness¶

  • Lateral quickness - cross-over step speed
  • Change of direction - split step reactivity
  • Endurance - maintain quality late in match
  • Core stability - balance during recovery

Mental Aspect¶

Recovery is a choice, not a reaction.

Decide before the point: "I will recover immediately after every shot." Make it automatic. The best players recover before the ball crosses the net.

Summary Checklist¶

After every shot: - [ ] Finish follow-through completely - [ ] Cross-over step FIRST - [ ] Side-shuffle to tactical center - [ ] Split step as opponent hits - [ ] Ready position: racket up, knees bent, eyes on ball
