Vital Groundwork¶

Good footwork is the foundation of every stroke. You cannot hit what you cannot reach.

Ready Position¶

The starting point for all movement:

  • Feet shoulder-width apart
  • Weight on balls of feet (not heels)
  • Knees flexed, athletic stance
  • Racket out front, centered
  • Eyes on opponent's contact
  • Relaxed but alert

Balance Principles¶

Principle Application
Wide base Stability for lateral moves
Low center Quick direction changes
Weight forward Ready to move any direction
Head still Visual stability

Movement Patterns¶

1. Split Step¶

The most important footwork move - timed with opponent's contact. - Small hop as opponent hits - Land on balls of feet - Legs wide, knees bent - Ready to push in any direction

2. First Step¶

Explosive initial movement: - Push off outside foot - Drive in direction of ball - Turn hips/shoulders simultaneously - Eyes stay on ball

3. Adjustment Steps¶

Small steps to fine-tune position: - Short, quick steps - Stay low - don't pop up - Racket preparing simultaneously - Stop in balanced position

4. Recovery¶

Return to ready position after stroke: - Cross-over step first - Side-shuffle back to center - Split step as opponent hits - Face net in ready position

Court Coverage Zones¶

Zone Distance Movement
Comfort 1-2 steps Adjustment steps
Stretch 3-4 steps Full sprint + adjust
Emergency 5+ steps Desperation, lob/defend

Common Footwork Errors¶

Error Consequence Fix
No split step Late reaction Split step every ball
Heels down Slow first step Stay on balls of feet
Crossing feet Off balance Side-shuffle, don't cross
Straight legs Can't change direction Keep knees bent
Late recovery Caught out of position Immediate recovery

Practice Drills¶

  1. Shadow movement - no ball, perfect patterns
  2. Cone drills - specific movement patterns
  3. Ball machine - random feeds, game-like
  4. Partner rally - focus on recovery
  5. Multi-ball - rapid feeds, quick feet
