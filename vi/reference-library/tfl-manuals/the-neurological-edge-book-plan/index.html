The Neurological Edge Book Plan¶

Source: The_Neurological_Edge_Book_Plan.docx

BOOK PLAN

The Neurological Edge

Inside the Science of Brain-Powered Performance, Recovery, and Human Potential in Tennis

Proposed subtitle

Inside the Science of Brain-Powered Performance, Recovery & Human Potential in Tennis

Genre

Narrative Nonfiction / Sports Science

Target audience

Tennis coaches, sports medicine professionals, elite athletes, performance scientists, serious recreational players

Estimated length

90,000 – 110,000 words across 18 chapters in 4 parts

Comparable titles

The Brain That Changes Itself (Doidge), The Talent Code (Coyle), Play On (Bowers)

Core argument

The brain is not a passenger in athletic performance — it is the engine, the governor, and the ultimate frontier of human potential in sport

Book Overview

Tennis has always been described as a physical game — of footwork, of muscle, of stamina. But the most consequential revolution in the sport is not happening in the gym or on the training court. It is happening inside the skull.

The Neurological Edge traces the emergence of a new discipline at the intersection of clinical sports medicine and high-performance cognitive training: the field of the tennis neurological specialist. Drawing on the latest research in neuroscience, quantum biology, neuroprotection, and elite coaching methodology, this book presents a comprehensive portrait of what it now means to optimize the human nervous system for competitive sport — and what that optimization reveals about the deeper nature of human performance.

The book moves across two intertwined tracks. The first is clinical: the sports neurologist who manages traumatic brain injuries, concussion protocols, and the long-term neurological health of athletes from junior players to aging professionals. The second is developmental: the neuro-performance coach who upgrades the healthy nervous system — training vision, balance, proprioception, and reaction speed — to unlock reserves of skill that traditional coaching cannot reach.

Woven through both tracks is a bigger question: if the brain governs every movement, every decision, and every recovery — then the athlete who masters their nervous system does not merely improve their game. They change who they are.

Key Topics from the Source Research

The following are the major intellectual territories the book covers, drawn directly from the Tennis Neurological Specialist research:

Clinical Sports Neurology

Sports neurology as a medical subspecialty — its evolution from collision sports into tennis

The 15+ year training pathway: bachelor's degree through sports neurology fellowship and CAQ certification

The multidisciplinary team model: neurologists, neuropsychologists, athletic trainers, orthopedic surgeons

Dr. Brian Hainline and the unification of neurological standards across Grand Slams, ATP, and WTA

The NCAA-DoD CARE Consortium — the largest concussion study in history and its cultural impact

Traumatic Brain Injury & Concussion Science

Mechanisms of concussion in tennis: falls, ball impacts, doubles collisions

Pathophysiology — chemical changes and axonal stretching in the brain

The 'Recognize and Remove' protocol as the current gold standard

Second Impact Syndrome: the catastrophic risk of premature return

The paradigm shift from 'cocooning' (dark room rest) to relative rest and early light activity

The 7-stage Return to Sport (RTS) protocol with symptom thresholds at each stage

Neurological Rehabilitation & Specialized Clinics

NeuroPeak (Surrey, BC): integrating physiotherapy with electromagnetics, vibroacoustics, and light therapy

The Shiftwave system: pulsed pressure waves that reset the autonomic nervous system

Activate Neurological Physiotherapy: Bobath method, PD Warrior, neuroplasticity-based rehabilitation

The two-way relationship between exercise and brain health — tennis as neuroprotective agent

Neuro-Performance Coaching

The neuro-centric model: why traditional technical coaching plateaus and what lies beyond it

The Input-Integration-Output framework: how sensory data becomes motor action

Sensory 'threat' responses and how the nervous system limits power to protect the body

Z-Health Performance: R-Phase, I-Phase, S-Phase, T-Phase, and the Master Practitioner path

How targeted neurological drills can transform performance in minutes rather than months

Neuroathletics

The Alexander Zverev model: brain training as a core component of elite tennis conditioning

The visual system: gaze stability, depth perception, VOR training for ball tracking at pace

The vestibular system: balance under rotational stress, sudden direction changes

The proprioceptive system: internal body mapping, closed-eye stroke training, kinetic chain awareness

Kinetic chain enhancements: barbell lunges, cable tricep work, heavy carries — the neuroathletic rationale

Neurocognitive Motor Control

Book Structure

The book is organized into four parts, each representing a distinct layer of the neurological framework — from the foundations of brain science in sport, through clinical care, to peak performance optimization, and finally toward the frontiers of what the brain may be capable of.

PART I

The Governing Brain

Why everything in tennis begins and ends with the nervous system

CH1

The Governor — Rethinking What Sport Is Actually About

Opens with a vivid match scene analyzed not through biomechanics but neural computation — every shot is a brain event. Establishes the core thesis: the CNS governs all athletic output. Introduces the two specialisms — clinical sports neurologist and neuro-performance coach — and why both are now essential.

CH2

The Fifteen-Year Road — Training a Sports Neurologist

Traces the education pathway from pre-med through fellowship. Uses Dr. Brian Hainline's career as the organizing biography — from CMO of the USTA to architect of the world's largest concussion study. Examines what is lost when a sport lacks this level of specialized medical oversight, and what changes when it gains it.

CH3

The Signal and the Threat — How the Nervous System Limits You

Introduces the Input-Integration-Output model and the concept of neurological 'threat.' Explains why a player's ceiling is often not muscular or technical but perceptual — the brain limiting output to protect the body from injury. The chapter establishes neuro-performance coaching as the discipline of clearing these threat signals.

PART II

When the Brain Gets Hurt

The science of concussion, recovery, and neuroprotection

CH4

Impact — The Physics and Biology of Concussion in Tennis

Details the mechanisms of TBI in a non-contact sport: court falls on hard surfaces, high-velocity ball impacts, doubles collisions. Explains the pathophysiology — ionic cascades, metabolic dysfunction, axonal microtrauma. Introduces the 'Recognize and Remove' protocol and Second Impact Syndrome.

CH5

The Dark Room Was Wrong — A Revolution in Recovery Science

Narrates the paradigm shift from cocooning (strict rest, zero stimulation) to relative rest with early reactivation. Uses research to show why immobility prolongs recovery and how light aerobic activity within 24–48 hours accelerates it. Walks through the 7-stage Return to Sport protocol with clinical nuance.

CH6

The Clinics — NeuroPeak, Activate, and the Frontier Rehab Centers

Takes the reader inside specialized neurological physiotherapy clinics in British Columbia and beyond. Examines NeuroPeak's vibroacoustic and electromagnetic therapies, the Bobath method at Activate, and the lifespan perspective — how rehab for Parkinson's and stroke recovery uses tennis-like movements to rebuild neural pathways.

CH7

The Culture of Brain Health — From 'Shake It Off' to Evidence-Based Care

Examines the cultural change in how concussion is treated across all levels of tennis. The Hainline effect on Grand Slam standards. The USTA and ITF regulatory frameworks for 2025. The ethical stakes of protecting junior players. The question of whether the sport has gone far enough.

PART III

Upgrading the Healthy Brain

Neuro-performance coaching, neuroathletics, and peak optimization

CH8

The Neuro-Centric Coach — Beyond Technique

Introduces neuro-performance coaching as a discipline distinct from both medicine and conventional instruction. Profiles the Z-Health curriculum — R-Phase through T-Phase — and what distinguishes a Master Practitioner. The central claim: most coaching plateaus happen not in the body but in the nervous system.

CH9

Eyes First — Visual Training for Ball Sport Mastery

Deep dive into the visual system as the primary sensory input for tennis performance. Gaze stability, depth perception, smooth pursuit, VOR calibration. The science of why elite players 'see' the ball differently — and the specific drills that close the gap. The thumb-tracking exercise and its effect on a forehand.

CH10

Balance and Body Map — The Vestibular and Proprioceptive Systems

Examines the vestibular system (inner ear, rotational acceleration, rapid directional change) and proprioception (internal limb mapping). Explains why players who close their eyes mid-drill often improve consistency. Practical protocols for upgrading foot-eye coordination and reducing kinesthetic errors at high pace.

CH11

The Zverev Model — Neuroathletics as Professional Practice

Uses Alexander Zverev's adoption of neuroathletics as a case study for how the world's top players are training the brain as rigorously as the body. Breaks down his conditioning program through a neuroathletic lens: lunges for neural activation of the posterior chain, carries for serve-chain integrity, cable drills for racket-path precision.

CH12

Reaction Time and the Millisecond Margin — Neurocognitive Training

Introduces the Tennis Center for Performance Research and the concept of neurocognitive motor control as the elite differentiator. Examines NeuroTracker and computerized light systems — how 3D object tracking improves decision speed. Explores cognitive load: the simultaneous processing demands of reading spin, position, and geometry in real time.

PART IV

The Frontier

Technologies, youth development, and the future of the neurological specialist

CH13

Retune the Brain — MeRT and Magnetic Resonance Therapy

Examines Magnetic e-Resonance Therapy (MeRT) — the use of TMS guided by quantitative EEG to identify and correct irregular brainwave patterns. Explores its application to focus, sleep quality, and cognitive clarity in athletes living across time zones under chronic performance stress. What it means to personalize brain treatment to an individual's neural rhythm.

CH14

Brain Vital Signs — The NeuroCatch Platform and Objective Measurement

Details the NeuroCatch Platform and the extraction of event-related potentials (ERPs) as objective brain metrics. The N100, P300, and N400 as windows into sensory speed, attention, and cognitive integration. Research from elite ice hockey mapped onto tennis. The possibility of pre-match and post-match neurological profiling as standard practice.

CH15

The X-Factor and the Genetic Ceiling — What Cannot Be Trained

An honest reckoning with the limits of neurological optimization. The TCPR research on height, limb length, and muscle fiber distribution as genetically determined factors. Where neuro training reaches its ceiling. The ethical question of neurological advantage — and whether elite sport is heading toward a brain-performance arms race.

CH16

The Junior Brain — Tennis as a Cognitive Development Tool

Reframes youth tennis as an executive function program. Evidence that children with one or more years of play outperform peers in working memory and cognitive flexibility. The 'open-ended' sport advantage. Richard Brice's Tennis Hacker as a model for brain-based junior coaching. The responsibility of coaches who shape developing nervous systems.

CH17

Safe Play and the Regulatory Brain — USTA, ITF, and the Ethics of Proximity

Examines the 2025 USTA Safe Play Proactive Policies and ITF health standards. The regulatory implications of recovery modalities that require physical proximity to athletes, especially minors. How neurological specialists — with intimate knowledge of brain and behavior — carry a unique safeguarding obligation.

CH18

The Synchronized Coach — Interpersonal Neural Synchrony and the Future

The book's culminating chapter: EEG hyperscanning research revealing brain-to-brain alignment between coach and athlete during effective instruction. The neuroscience of trust, attunement, and the 'flow' state in a coaching relationship. The vision of the tennis neurological specialist of the future — one who optimizes not just the individual brain, but the relational field between athlete and team.

Editorial Features & Special Elements

Each chapter is designed with the following recurring structural elements to maintain reader engagement and reinforce key concepts:

Feature

Description

Opening scene

Each chapter opens with a vivid scene — a match moment, a clinical encounter, a training session — that grounds abstract science in lived experience

The science

A clear, accessible explanation of the underlying neuroscience, grounded in peer-reviewed research and clinical data

The practitioner

Profiles of real specialists, coaches, researchers, and athletes who embody the chapter's concepts

The protocol

Practical frameworks — training drills, clinical checklists, assessment tools — that readers can apply or reference

The frontier

Each chapter closes by gesturing toward open questions, emerging research, and the next horizon of the topic

Key terms sidebar

A concise glossary of neurological terminology introduced in the chapter for non-specialist readers

Market Positioning & Audience

Primary readership

Sports medicine professionals, physical therapists, and clinical neurologists working with athletes

Tennis coaches at the professional, collegiate, and high-performance junior levels

Performance scientists, sports psychologists, and strength and conditioning specialists

Elite and serious recreational tennis players seeking a competitive edge beyond physical training

Secondary readership

Neuroscience enthusiasts and readers of popular science books on brain performance

Parents of competitive junior tennis players interested in cognitive and neurological development

Broader sports science readers — the book's frameworks are transferable to other racket and precision sports

Competitive differentiation

No existing book addresses the tennis neurological specialist as a unified discipline spanning clinical medicine and performance coaching. Most sports neurology literature is academic and practitioner-facing. Most tennis coaching books ignore neuroscience entirely. This book occupies the unexplored space between those worlds, written with the narrative accessibility of the best sports science popular nonfiction.
