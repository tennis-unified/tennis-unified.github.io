Starting Positions¶

Your starting position determines what shots you can reach and how quickly.

Baseline Position¶

Singles¶

  • 2-3 feet behind baseline
  • Centered on hash mark (deuce) or slightly to ad side (ad)
  • Adjust based on opponent's tendencies
  • Deeper vs big hitters, closer vs pushers

Doubles¶

  • Server's partner: on service line, near center
  • Receiver's partner: on service line, poaching position
  • Receiver: 2-3 feet behind baseline

Mid-Court Position¶

Approach Position¶

  • Inside baseline after attacking shot
  • Split step as opponent hits
  • Move forward diagonally to net
  • Racket up, ready to volley

Transition Zone ("No Man's Land")¶

  • Avoid staying here - vulnerable to passing shots
  • Move through quickly to net or back
  • Split step if caught here

Net Position¶

Offensive Volley Position¶

  • 3-4 feet from net
  • Centered on likely return
  • Racket up at chest height
  • Knees bent, weight forward

Defensive Volley Position¶

  • 6-8 feet from net
  • Deeper vs big hitters
  • Cover lobs first
  • React to volleys

Positioning by Shot¶

Your Shot Next Position
Deep rally ball Recover to baseline center
Short ball (attack) Move in, approach position
Approach shot Close to net, volley position
Serve Baseline (serve) / Service line (partner)
Return Baseline center / Move in on weak serve
Lob Back to baseline / Chase down
Overhead Move forward to net

Reading Opponent¶

Adjust position based on: - Favorite shots - shade to their strength - Patterns - anticipate crosscourt/dtl - Weaknesses - force them to weak side - Tendencies - serve patterns, return patterns

Court Geometry¶

Angles¶

  • Crosscourt = longer, safer, more angle
  • Down the line = shorter, riskier, changes direction
  • Inside-out = forehand from backhand side

Recovery¶

  • Recover to center of possible returns
  • Not geometric center - tactical center
  • Factor in your shot placement

Practice Drills¶

  1. Shadow positioning - move to correct spots
  2. Point play with position rules - enforce recovery
  3. Video analysis - check positioning
  4. Pattern drills - specific sequences
  5. Match simulation - realistic positioning
