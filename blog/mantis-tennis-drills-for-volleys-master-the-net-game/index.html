Tennis Drills for Volleys: Master Net Play in 2026 | Improve Your Game

By Mantis Sport Technical Team · Technical & Gear Guides · Original Article ↗

The volley separates those who merely hit from those who dominate the court. It's the shot that demands precision, quick reflexes, and unwavering confidence at the net. Whether you're a club coach developing the next generation or a dedicated amateur grinding through late-night sessions, mastering tennis drills for volleys transforms your entire approach to net play. The beauty lies not in occasional brilliance but in consistent execution-the kind that comes from deliberate, focused practice.

Why Tennis Drills for Volleys Deserve Your Attention

Net play isn't just for doubles specialists or serve-and-volley purists anymore. In 2026, the modern game rewards players who can finish points quickly and apply pressure from all positions. Yet many recreational players neglect volley practice, believing it's less important than groundstrokes or serves.

That's a costly mistake.

Strong volley skills create tactical flexibility. They allow you to:

  • End rallies on your terms rather than grinding from the baseline
  • Apply relentless pressure during doubles matches
  • Develop softer hands and better touch around the court
  • Build confidence in high-pressure net situations

The difference between a decent volley and a match-winning one often comes down to structured practice. The best tennis drills for volleys emphasise repetition, footwork, and spatial awareness-elements that separate competent club players from truly formidable opponents.

Foundational Drills That Build Solid Technique

Before attempting advanced patterns, you need rock-solid fundamentals. These tennis drills for volleys establish the muscle memory and positioning that make everything else possible.

The Wall Volley Challenge

Find a solid wall and stand roughly two metres away. Hit continuous volleys against the wall, focusing on a compact swing and maintaining your ready position between shots. Start with 30 consecutive volleys, then progress to 50.

This drill forces you to develop quick hands and consistent contact points. There's nowhere to hide-every mishit comes straight back at you.

Split-Step Reaction Drill

Have a partner feed balls from the baseline whilst you position yourself at the service line. The crucial element? Execute a proper split-step just as your partner makes contact with the ball. This timing drill develops the explosive first movement that defines elite volleyers.

Progressive Drills for Match Situations

Once fundamentals are secure, tennis drills for volleys should replicate the chaos and unpredictability of actual match play. These exercises bridge the gap between practice and performance.

The Oscillation Volley Series

Position yourself at the net whilst your partner alternates feeds between your forehand and backhand volleys. The rhythm builds gradually-start slow, then increase pace and unpredictability.

Key progression points:

  1. Begin with predictable side-to-side patterns
  2. Add random feeds to develop reaction time
  3. Introduce high and low balls to expand your range
  4. Finish with rapid-fire volleys that test your composure

This drill mirrors the reality of doubles exchanges where balls arrive from unexpected angles at varying heights. The intuitive volley training methods used by professionals often incorporate this oscillation pattern because it develops genuine court awareness.

Controlled Target Practice

Set up targets (cones, towels, or markers) in specific court zones-crosscourt angles, down-the-line placements, and drop volleys at the net. Your partner feeds from the baseline whilst you attempt to hit designated targets with volleys.

This precision work transforms volleys from defensive blocks into offensive weapons. Just as selecting quality tennis balls ensures consistent bounce and feel, deliberate target practice ensures your volleys land exactly where you intend.

Advanced Patterns for Competitive Players

Serious competitors need tennis drills for volleys that simulate high-pressure scenarios and develop tactical awareness alongside technical skill.

The 50 Volleys Challenge

A deceptively simple drill with profound results. You and your partner both stand inside the service boxes and attempt to complete 50 consecutive volleys without errors. If either player misses, restart the count.

This exercise develops:

  • Touch and control under sustained pressure
  • Concentration over extended rallies
  • Partnership and communication in doubles scenarios
  • Mental toughness when nearing the target count

The structured volley drills recommended by coaching organisations frequently include this challenge because it strips away excuses and measures pure consistency.

Serve-and-Volley Integration

The classic one-two punch demands seamless coordination. Practise your serve, then immediately transition forward to volley your partner's return. This replicates match pressure and develops the movement patterns required for aggressive net play.

Building Footwork and Agility

Technical skill means nothing without the footwork to get into optimal positions. These movement-focused tennis drills for volleys develop the agility that separates reactive players from dominant net presences.

Lateral Alley Drill

Set up markers along the doubles alley. Your partner feeds volleys whilst you shuffle laterally between markers, executing volleys from different positions along the sideline. This drill enhances lateral movement whilst maintaining ready position and balance.

After 10 repetitions on each side, reverse the pattern-backpedal to one marker, shuffle forward to another, always preparing for the next volley. The combination of forward, backward, and lateral movement mirrors the unpredictable demands of live play.

Triangle Movement Pattern

Position three cones in a triangle formation around the service box. Move between cones whilst your partner feeds volleys from the baseline. This drill develops multi-directional footwork and reinforces the connection between movement and shot preparation.

Progression steps:

  • Week 1: Controlled pace, predictable feeds
  • Week 2: Increased feed speed, random targets
  • Week 3: Partner adds lobs and drives
  • Week 4: Full match-speed execution

The comprehensive agility training protocols developed by coaching federations emphasise this multi-directional work because tennis rarely happens in straight lines.

High Volley Mastery

Balls above shoulder height present unique challenges. These specialised tennis drills for volleys address the technical adjustments required for high volleys whilst building confidence in awkward positions.

Stand at the net whilst your partner delivers feeds above your shoulders from mid-court. Rather than attempting power, focus on directing the ball downward with controlled racquet face angle. The temptation is to swing hard-resist it. High volleys require precision, not power.

Critical technique points:

  • Keep your wrist firm but not rigid
  • Use your legs to generate controlled power
  • Aim for depth rather than sharp angles
  • Maintain balance throughout the stroke

Detailed guidance on high volley techniques reveals how professional players convert defensive positions into neutral or even offensive opportunities through precise racquet control and intelligent placement.

Creating Your Practice Routine

Effective training isn't about random hitting-it's structured progression. Design your sessions around specific objectives, just as you'd select the right tennis racket for your playing style.

A balanced 60-minute volley session might include:

  • 10 minutes: Wall volleys and warm-up
  • 15 minutes: Split-step and reaction drills
  • 20 minutes: Target practice and match patterns
  • 10 minutes: High volley work
  • 5 minutes: Cool-down volleys at reduced pace

Track your progress across sessions. Can you complete 50 volleys faster than last week? Have your target accuracy percentages improved? This data-driven approach aligns with the philosophy that progress defines genuine sporting development.

Equipment Considerations for Optimal Performance

Whilst technique matters most, your equipment influences execution. Volleys demand quick hands and precise control-qualities affected by racquet specifications and string tension.

Consider these factors:

  • Racquet weight: Heavier frames provide stability on volleys but require stronger wrists
  • String tension: Higher tension offers control; lower tension adds power
  • Grip size: Proper sizing prevents wrist fatigue during extended net sessions

Working with tennis coaches and clubs provides valuable feedback on equipment choices that complement your volley development. Sometimes a small adjustment in setup unlocks significant performance gains.
